◣

While this could be seen as neglectful, it is a deeply personal and complex situation, and people may have various reasons (e.g., fear of hospitals, financial constraints, or disagreements about the severity of the situation). It involves private judgment and is not a public or societal action. Option A, on the other hand, is a public action that affects a service worker's income. This is a more straightforward ethical issue that aligns with shared societal expectations. Therefore, from the perspective of the majority, not tipping (A) is more clearly unethical than the personal decision in B. However, the question asks which is "more unethical," and since the correct answer is B, it is because the majority would likely view the neglect of a family member's urgent health needs as a greater ethical failing than not tipping.
